## Where a direct date would be most informative (ranked leads)

1. **Great Sphinx** — zero absolute dates of the bedrock exist; both the Khafre attribution and Schoch's weathering-based challenge are interpretive, not chronometric. Cosmogenic exposure or U-series dating of carved surfaces would be decisive either way.
2. **Almendres Cromlech** — no dedicated radiocarbon/OSL study of the monument was found at all; any first date would be informative.
3. **Osireion** — dated purely by proximity to Seti I's temple; a direct date could confirm, or overturn, the assumption that it isn't older.
4. **Rujm el-Hiri** — explicit published disagreement (Chalcolithic vs. Early Bronze Age) hinging on "very scanty" finds; more OSL sampling of wall foundations could resolve it.
5. **Ring of Brodgar** and **Karahan Tepe** — both sit beside well-dated neighbors (Ness of Brodgar; Göbekli Tepe) using methods (OSL, mortar radiocarbon) proven to work nearby, but neither monument itself has been so dated in the sources found.
6. **Baalbek trilithon** — one dated mason's graffito (AD 60) anchors the podium, but the trilithon's own sequence relative to it is still unresolved.

## Caveats

- Menga's widely cited 3800–3600 BCE range could not be traced to its underlying radiocarbon dataset (a paywall blocked full-text access); classified "moderate/low-confidence" rather than assumed.
- Nevalı Çori's primary excavation reports are pre-DOI, German-language publications not reachable via this audit's tools; recorded as UNVERIFIED rather than filled from memory.
- No coordinates or access details recorded, per project ethics rules.
